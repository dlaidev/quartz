# Blog writing conventions

This repository contains the entries published at https://mlai.blog.

Use tight, direct, K&R-style technical prose. State the mechanism, evidence, or consequence. Preserve technical accuracy and the author's claims. Do not invent anecdotes, sources, results, or personality to make prose sound human.

Apply these rules when drafting or editing posts. Do not rewrite the existing archive unless asked. Before returning a draft, review the prose for the patterns below and rewrite rhetorical filler into concrete statements or delete it.

## Avoid rhetorical tics

- Repeated negation chains: "No X, no Y" and "did not X, did not Y" (including contractions).
- "Don't VERB it ... VERB it" reversals, such as "Don't call it X. Call it Y."
- "That's the whole ...", "is the whole ...", "is the entire ...", and "the entire ... is" declarations about the point, trick, game, idea, or business model.
- "Sit with that/this/it" and "sit with the discomfort" invitations.
- "You already know."
- "The X is real, and/not ..." and "is the real ... and it" rhetorical framing.
- "The punchline is", "the punchline:", and "the punchline?"
- "Worth naming" and "it's worth naming that."
- "That's not nothing" and its this/it/which variants.
- Consecutive sentences built on an echoing skeleton.
- Announced sincerity: "I won't pretend", "I'll be honest", "let's be honest", "to be clear", and opening "Honestly," or "Look,".
- "That's the part ...", "the part that makes me trust ...", and "my favourite part ..." gestures.
- "The only X I trust", "the only thing it needs", and "the only X that matters" reveals.
- "Don't take my word for it" and related invitations.
- "Turns out" and "it turns out that" revelation openers.
- Simplicity slogans: "fits in your head", "small enough to hold in your head", "batteries included", "it just works", "zero config", and "sane defaults."
- Stacked rhetorical questions.
- Three or more consecutive sentences repeating the same opening word or phrase for effect.
- Forced colon-introduced triples. Keep genuine technical enumerations when the items are necessary; this pattern is a review cue rather than a blanket ban.
- Staged reveals: "here's the twist/thing/catch/kicker/rub" and "here's the first example."
- "X is dead" and "long live X" headlines.
- "That's why X mattered" and "this is why X mattered" significance claims.
- Stranded auxiliary contrasts, such as "The tool died; the data didn't" or "Reading passed. Writing didn't."

## Avoid stock AI prose

- Habitual inflated vocabulary: delve, tapestry, meticulous, pivotal, intricate, interplay, underscore, garner, bolster, vibrant, bustling, multifaceted, seamless, ever-evolving.
- Negative parallelism: "not just X, but Y", "not only ... but ...", "not X, but Y", "it's not X — it's Y", and "isn't X. It's Y."
- Didactic preambles: "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "it should be noted", and "worth pausing/considering/asking."
- "Stands/serves as a testament/reminder" and "is a testament to."
- "Plays a crucial/pivotal/vital/key/significant role."
- "Ever-evolving/changing/shifting landscape" and "in today's fast-paced world."
- Unnamed authority: "experts argue", "some critics have noted", "observers suggest", and "industry reports indicate." Cite a specific source when attribution is needed.
- Formulaic outlook: "despite these challenges", "faces several challenges", "challenges remain", "remains to be seen", and "time will tell." State the actual limitation or unresolved question.
- Superficial participle tails: ", highlighting/underscoring/showcasing/reflecting ...". Explain the consequence directly when supported.
- Promotional language: "nestled in", "in the heart of", "rich tapestry/heritage", "hidden gem", "boasts a", "breathtaking", and "stunning views."
- Chatbot leftovers: "as an AI language model", "as of my last update", knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, oaicite/contentReference/turn0search markers, and utm_source tracking parameters in links.

## Review boundaries

Use judgment for literal technical terms, quotations, code, and factual comparisons. For example, "real time" and "real estate" are not rhetorical uses of "real"; an underscore can be a literal character. Preserve necessary distinctions and explicit uncertainty. Do not replace a banned slogan with a different slogan or obscure a technical statement merely to evade a phrase match.
